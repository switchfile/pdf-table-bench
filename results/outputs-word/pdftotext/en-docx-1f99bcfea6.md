Formulation, Optimization, And Evaluation Of Feverfew (Tanacetum Parthenium)
Extract Tablets For Enhanced Drug Release And Stability
Isha Narang - Research Scholar
Saloni Saw - Research Scholar
Department of Pharmacy, Kalinga University, Raipur, Chhattisgarh
Sandeep Prasad Tiwari – Associate Professor
Faculty of Pharmacy, Kalinga University, Raipur, Chhattisgarh
ABSTRACT:
Migraine is a common neurological condition that needs to be treated with effective and safe therapeutic
options. The aim of the present study was to formulate, optimize and evaluate tablets of Feverfew (Tanacetum
parthenium) extract that will improve drug release and stability. Preformulation analyses revealed that the
extract was suitable with acceptable physicochemical properties and λmax of 235 nm to be used in quantitative
analysis. Tablets were formulated using the wet granulation method and different concentrations of binding
agents (F1 -F7) were used to prepare the tablets and measured in terms of pre- and post-compression
parameters. All the formulations showed good flow characteristics, mechanical strength, and consistency.
Dissolution in vitro studies indicated that there existed considerable variation in the drug release profile with
formulation F7 exhibiting the highest cumulative drug release (90.76% at 120 min). The optimized formulation
(F7) showed better performance than the marketed formulation (Himalaya Mentat Tablets), and the
dissolution efficiency was improved. Kinetics of drug release F7 was shown to follow a zero-order kinetics (R
2 = 0.99) with a non-Fickian diffusion step. The stability studies under ICH conditions proved that the
optimized formulation is stable without any significant changes in physicochemical parameters. The final
formulation developed had a better drug release and stability, which suggests that this might be a good oral
dosage form of migraine management.
Keywords: Feverfew; Tanacetum parthenium; Wet granulation; Tablet formulation; Drug release kinetics;
Dissolution study; Stability study; Migraine

1. Introduction
Migraine is a chronic, disabling neurovascular disorder with recurrent episodes of moderate to severe headache
associated with nausea, photophobia and phonophobia with a significant proportion of the global population
being affected and adding to disease burden [1]. It has a pathophysiology of activation of the trigeminovascular
system and release of neuropeptides including calcitonin gene-related peptide (CGRP), which results in
neurogenic inflammation and vascular response alterations [2]. Even though the use of conventional therapy
such as the use of non-steroidal anti-inflammatory drugs, triptans and prophylactic agents are widely
employed, there are usually limitations in clinical utility due to delayed effect of action, gastrointestinal
irritation, systemic adverse effects, and lack of compliance in patients [3]. As a result, increased attention is
being drawn towards herbal therapeutics as alternative, less toxic and multi-targeted [4]. One well-established
medicinal herb in the treatment of migraines is Tanacetum parthenium (Feverfew), which is most likely to be
attributed to its bioactive component parthenolide, possesses anti-inflammatory, anti-serotonergic and
neuroprotective properties through inhibition of the production of prostaglandins and the regulation of
inflammatory pathways [5]. Nevertheless, conventional Feverfew formulations including powders and
capsules are linked to a sluggish effect, irregular dosing and poor drug release, in part, because of the low
aqueous solubility of parthenolide. Consequently, it is crucial to formulate an optimized version of tablet
formulation by use of appropriate excipients and processing methods to improve on drug release and
therapeutic performance [6]. In this regard, the current paper is devoted to the design, optimization, and testing
of Feverfew extract pills, and the comparative analysis of the results with a commercially available formulation
to determine the better efficacy and stability of the formulation.

4. Materials and Methods
4.1 Materials
Feverfew (Tanacetum parthenium) was used as the active pharmaceutical ingredient. Polyvinylpyrrolidone
(PVP K30) and starch were used as agents to bind, and microcrystalline cellulose (MCC) was used as a diluent,
lactose as filler, talc as glidant, and magnesium stearate as lubricant. All the chemicals, reagents and solvents
were used and they were of an analytical grade.
4.2 Preformulation Studies
Pre-formulation studies have been designed to investigate the physicochemical characteristics of Feverfew
extract as well as to determine the feasibility of Feverfew extract in formulation development. Depending on
the organoleptic properties, such as color, odour, and appearance, the visual perception was utilized to evaluate
the properties. Loss on drying was calculated by weighing the sample accurately and drying the sample at a
controlled temperature until a constant weight of both the sample and the moisture balance was obtained. The
Thiele tube technique was used to determine the melting point by putting the sample in a capillary tube and
recording the temperature at which the sample was completely melted. A calibrated digital pH meter was used
to determine the pH of 1% w/v solution. The maximum absorption wavelength (λ-max) of the active
constituent was determined through a scan in an appropriate wavelength range on a UV- Visible
spectrophotometer. By working up standard solutions of different concentrations and recording their value of
absorbance at 690 nm a calibration curve was drawn. Equilibrium shaking after followed by filtration and
analysis solubility studies were carried out in various solvents. To measure the flow and compressibility
properties, standard methods were used to determine the following micromeritic properties: angle of repose,
bulk density, tapped density, compressibility index and Hausner ratio. As the analysis method, optical
microscopy of a specific number of particles was performed to obtain the average particle size. The thin layer
chromatography (TLC) was done by adding silica gel-coated plates with an appropriate mobile phase and then
development of the prepared plates under appropriate conditions was performed to ensure the presence of the
active constituent [5,6,7].

4.4 Method of Preparation (Wet Granulation)

The wet granulation process was used to prepare tablets because the wet granulation technique enhances flow
conditions and compressibility of a powder mixture. The correct weight of Feverfew extract, lactose and
microcrystalline cellulose (MCC) were mixed thoroughly to produce a homogenous mixture.
Polyvinylpyrrolidone (PVP K30) and starch were then added to the powder base in a suitable solvent system,
and added in a controlled manner to the powder mix. To create granules of uniform size that could be
subsequently dried in a hot air oven at controlled temperature to eliminate residual moisture, the wet mass was
then filtered through a sieve. Dried granules were further sieved to fine granules to break up aggregates and
fine-tune the distribution of particle sizes. Glident and lubricant were then dry mixed (without over-lubricating

4.5 Pre-compression Evaluation of Granules
To determine their behavioral characteristics in regard to flowability and compressibility, standard procedures
were carried out to establish the suitability of the prepared granules in terms of compressibility and flowability.
To measure flow behavior, the fixed funnel technique was used to determine the angle of repose. Bulk density
was determined by finding the volume to which a known mass of granules would settle without tapping, and
tapped density by determining the volume to which a known mass of granules would settle without tapping
the cylinder and its tapped density. The compressibility index (index of Carr) was also calculated based on the
bulk and tapped density values, to determine the flowability of the granules, and the ratio of Hausner was
calculated as an extra parameter to show the interparticle friction and flow properties [9].
44.6 Post-compression Evaluation of Tablets
The pre-made tablets were tested under different post-compression conditions to guarantee their quality and
consistency. The weight variation was measured by achieving the average weight by weighing the necessary
number of tablets separately using an analytical balance and then calculating the average weight. The
uniformity of dimensions was tested by measuring the thickness of tablet with a calibrated Vernier calliper.
To determine hardness, a suitable hardness tester was used by measuring the force needed to perform the
diametric breakage of the tablets, which signifies the mechanical strength of the tablets. Assessment of
friability level was done by passing pre-weighed pills through a fixed number of rotations in a Roche
friabilator, followed by dedusting and calculations of the percent weight loss to determine the resistance to
abrasion. Uniformity of drug content was identified by weighing and powdering the drugs in a tablet-form as
accurately as possible and then preparing the proper dilutions and analyzing the samples using a UV-Visible
spectrophotometer at the predetermined λmax [10].
4.7 In-vitro Dissolution Study
To investigate dissolution behavior of the prepared tablets, in-vitro dissolution studies were conducted in the
USP Type II (paddle) dissolution apparatus. The medium of dissolution was kept at a controlled temperature
of 37±0.5 o C and stir at a constant rotational speed. Tablets were put into the dissolution medium, and samples

4.8 Optimization of Formulation
The formulation was optimized using the comparison of the prepared formulations on their physicochemical
properties, mechanical strength and in-vitro drug release behavior. The formulation with the most desirable
property in terms of uniformity, stability, drug release profile, was chosen as the optimized formulation.
4.9 Comparison with Marketed Formulation
The optimized formulation was put against a commercially available marketed product, under the same
experimental conditions. Both formulations were evaluated on the same evaluation parameters, such as the
physicochemical properties and in vitro dissolution studies, to determine comparative performance and drugs
release characteristics.
4.10 Drug Release Kinetics Study
In-vitro dissolution data representing the optimised formulation acquired were fitted into different
mathematical models such as zero-order, first-order, Higuchi, and Korsmeyer-Peppas models to assess the
release profile by kinetic and identity mechanisms. The suitability of each model was evaluated using the
values of correlation coefficient of the obtaining of the corresponding plots [12, 13].
4.11 Stability Studies
To test the impact of environmental factors on physicochemical attributes of the optimized formulation,
stability studies were performed. The tablets were kept at a pre-determined temperature and humidity
conditions and samples were withdrawn after a determined time to evaluate factors like physical appearance,
hardness and drug content in order to determine how stable a formulation is over time [13].

5. Results and Discussion
5.1 Preformulation Studies
Preformulation experiments addressed that Feverfew extract can be formulated as tablets, and the
pyrochemical properties were summarized to provide Table 2. The extract displayed usual characteristics of
herbs with a brown colour and distinct smell. The low moisture content means that there is little residual water,
which implies excellent stability, and limited chances of degradation. The small range of melting point
indicates an acceptable level of purity of the extract, and the near-neutral pH indicates that the extract is
compatible with excipients and that the extract can be administered orally. It was observed that parthenolide
had the maximum absorbance at 235 nm at which the wavelength was deemed suitable to make a quantitative
estimation as illustrated in Figure 1 UV visible Absorption Spectrum of Parthenolide. The solubility analysis
indicated that the extract is virtually insoluble in the aqueous media but exhibited a higher solubility in the
organic solvents, which indicated the lipophilic nature of the active constituent and the need of formulation
strategies to promote increased solubility. Micromeritic analysis revealed satisfactory flow properties but the
compressibility property indicates that there is need to granulate to enhance processing performance and ease
of handling. The particle size scale indicated a homogeneous distribution, which is good in terms of
homogenous mixing as well as homogenous release of drugs. In addition, a lay of the active constituent was
confirmed by thin layer chromatography as reflected by the appearance of specific spots under the UV light
in Figure 2 Thin Layer Chromatography (TLC) Profile of Feverfew Extract under UV Light which validated
the identification and quality of the extract.

5.5 Optimization of Formulation
After thorough consideration of pre- and post-compression parameters, in-vitro dissolution profiles revealed
formulation F7 as the optimized formulation. F7 was the one that displayed the highest cumulative drug release
without any compromised physicochemical properties, such as sufficient hardness, friability, and uniformity
of drug content. The increase in performance of F7 is due to the optimization of the concentration of binders
that not only formed the desired granules but also did not affect the disintegration and dissolution properties.
These results have shown that formulation variables are crucial in achieving the desired drug release
behaviour/mechanical strength.

5.7 Drug Release Kinetics
The drug release kinetics of the optimized formulation (F7) were measured by fitting drug dissolution data to
various mathematical models and graphical representation of the model was provided in Figure 6 Drug Release
Kinetic Plots of Optimized Formulation (F7). The maximum correlation coefficient of the zero-order model
(R2 = 0.99 ) was found to be the highest of all the formulations where the results were analyzed using
correlation coefficients. Comparatively, the first-order model demonstrated lesser correlation (R2 = 0.82)
indicating that the incidence of releasing drug was not concentrated largely on concentration. The Higuchi
model was shown to be very linear (R2 = 0.86) demonstrating that diffusion significantly contributed to the
release mechanism. More so, the correlation coefficient (R2 = 0.90) in the Korsmeye-Peppas model is
relatively high suggesting a non-Fickian (anomalous) type of transport mechanism whereby drug release is
governed by a combination of diffusion and erosion processes. These results validated the usefulness of the
developed formulation in maintaining a controlled and predictable release profile in facilitating sustained
therapeutic action.

6. Conclusion
The current researched formulated and optimized the Feverfew ( Tanacetum parthenium ) extract in the form
of tablets using the wet granulation technique. Preformulation studies had investigated the appropriateness of
the extract in tablets formulation, and all prepared batches had acceptable physicochemical properties, and met
the Pharmacopoeial standards. F7 showed better results having greatest percentage of drug release (90.76% at
120 minutes) and good mechanical properties. Comparative analysis showed that the optimized formulation
had a better dissolution behavior as compared to the product in the market (Himalaya Mentat Tablets), which
signified high therapeutic potential. A zero-order release profile was confirmed using drug release kinetics and
has a non-Fickian diffusion mechanism, which implied controlled and predictable drug release. Stability tests
also identified that the optimized formulation was stable in ICH conditions. Overall, the formulated
formulation presents an effective and stable oral dosage drug form with better drug release properties and it is
therefore noticed that it is a winning product in the approach to manage migraine.
