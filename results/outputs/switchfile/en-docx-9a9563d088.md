ABSTRACT:

The concept of represents an approach that has demonstrated financial and environmental sustainability in pharmaceutical analysis. Traditional approaches to analysis, such as High-Performance Liquid Chromatography (HPLC), Gas Chromatography (GC), and other standard methods of extraction, often involve the use of toxic substances, high-energy costs, and production of significant amounts of chemicals. In its essence, green analytical chemistry attempts to reduce negative environmental impacts while preserving analytical precision, accuracy, and compliance with regulations. This paper examines the concept of GAC, the application of green solvents, green approaches to chromatography, microextraction, greenness assessment methods, and their usage in pharmaceutical analysis.

Keywords: Green analytical chemistry, pharmaceutical analysis, green solvents, AGREE, GAPI.

1. Introduction

Analytical chemistry plays a crucial part in the pharmaceutical industry by ensuring proper identification, quantification, and quality control of medicines. Traditionally, analytical methods such as HPLC, GC, and classic extraction techniques continue to be widely used in the pharmaceutical industry. On the other hand, these methods usually involve using harmful organic solvents, having high energy consumption, and generating unwanted chemical waste that may negatively affect people's health and nature.

Green Chemistry focuses on minimizing hazardous components involved in chemical processes. At the same time, GAC promotes the application of similar concepts to analytical procedures with the main purpose of creating sustainable analytical techniques reducing the amount of solvents, energy consumed, and generation of waste while ensuring high accuracy, precision, and reliability of the results obtained.

The increasing focus on sustainability in the pharmaceutical industry has led researchers to create novel analytical techniques that could replace traditional analytical methods. Thus, today the pharmaceutical industry uses UHPLC, CE, SFC, and green spectroscopy techniques as alternatives to traditional analytical methods. In addition, such evaluation tools as AGREE, GAPI, NEMI, and Eco-scale help to assess greenness of analytical procedures.

Thus, the current paper is aimed at discussing the principles, techniques, application areas, greenness evaluation tools, challenges, and future directions in GAC.

2. PRINCIPLES

Concept is based on some ideas that help reduce the harm that laboratory work can cause to the environment. At the time the GAC makes sure that the results of the laboratory work are good and reliable.

The main ideas of the GAC include using harmful chemicals making less waste saving energy and keeping people safe in the laboratory.

The GAC has twelve ideas that can help people create better ways of doing laboratory work especially when it comes to testing medicines. The GAC principles are useful for creating analytical methods, in pharmaceutical analysis and people can use the GAC principles to make these methods better

#### 2.1 Direct Techniques

Direct analytical techniques should be applied wherever possible because such techniques require less complicated sample treatment. This will result in the minimization of the consumption of organic solvents, analysis time, and waste generation. Moreover, contamination risk will be minimized due to direct analytical procedures.

<!-- page 3 -->

#### 2.2 Minute quantity of Samples and Minimization of Sample Number

The key principle of green analytical techniques is the use of the minimal amount of samples possible without compromising analytical accuracy. As it was mentioned above, a reduction in the number of samples allows reducing chemical use and waste generation. Miniaturized analytical methods will be beneficial to meet this principle.

#### 2.3 In-situ Analysis

In-situ analysis implies measuring samples without moving them to the laboratory or any other premises. The usage of portable devices is becoming more popular among environmentally oriented researchers.

#### 2.4 Integration of Analytical Operations

This principle suggests integrating analytical processes to reduce reagent use, minimize energy consumption, and save time.

#### 2.5 Automation and Miniaturization of Analytical Procedures

Automation and miniaturization minimize the amount of solvents and chemicals required for analysis. The application of miniaturized equipment results in a lower volume of waste generation and energy consumption. Automation of analytical processes increases precision and improves laboratory safety.

#### 2.6 Avoidance of Derivatization

Derivatization often requires the usage of additional chemicals and leads to an increase in waste production. Green analytical chemistry suggests avoiding derivatization wherever it is possible to make analytical procedures more eco-friendly.

#### 2.7 Reduction of Waste Production

One of the main principles of GAC is the minimization of waste generated by analytical processes. Proper waste management procedures and the application of environmentally friendly chemicals will help achieve this goal.

<!-- page 4 -->

#### 2.8 Multi-analyte Techniques

Multi-analyte techniques should be applied whenever it is possible because multi-analyte analysis requires shorter analytical time, lower solvent consumption, and less energy compared to one-component analysis.

#### 2.9 Energy Saving

Principles of green chemistry strive for lower energy utilization in processes ranging from sample preparation, separation, to detection. Energy savings through energy-efficient instrumentation and faster analysis time improve overall sustainability.

#### 2.10 Renewable Reagents

In situations where feasible, renewable or biodegradable compounds should be used. For example, bio-solvents like ethanol and ethyl lactate are considered environmentally friendly solvents compared to toxic organic solvents.

#### 2.11 Alternative To Toxic Reagents

Wherever possible, toxic reagents should be replaced by reagents that pose fewer health risks. Such alternatives reduce hazards in the laboratory setting, making work safer for workers.

#### 2.12 Improved Human Health

Laboratory operations should involve minimal contact with hazardous chemicals. Such practices improve the health status of laboratory staff members.

All these principles have significantly influenced the development of pharmaceutical analytical procedures. Green Analytical Chemistry ensures sustainability of the environment and laboratories.

4. GREEN ANALYTICAL TECHNIQUES IN PHARMACEUTICAL ANALYSIS

Green analytical techniques refer to environmentally sustainable methods aimed at reducing the amount of solvent used, minimizing waste, lowering energy cost, while maintaining accuracy in the analysis and increasing environmental safety.

#### 4.1 Green HPLC

The green form of HPLC entails substituting toxic solvents such as methanol and acetonitrile with ethanol and water. Additionally, it uses low flow rates and small column lengths to reduce solvent use and generate minimal waste.

Advantages

- Reduction of the use of toxic solvents
- Decreased waste
- Increased safety
- Inexpensive analytical procedures

#### 4.2 UHPLC

UHPLC can be described as a green evolution of HPLC which utilizes columns with smaller particles to conduct faster analyses.

Advantages

<!-- page 7 -->

- Fast analysis
- Decreased use of solvent
- Improved peak resolution
- Less energy used

#### 4.3 Capillary Electrophoresis (CE)

The green aspect of CE is attributed to the low amounts of samples and solvent required for the analysis.

#### Advantages

- Minimal solvent use
- Rapid analysis
- Less waste produced
- High peak resolutio

#### 4.4 Supercritical Fluid Chromatography (SFC)

SFC refers to a green analytical method involving the use of supercritical fluid carbon dioxide in place of organic solvents.

#### Advantages

- Minimal use of organic solvent
- Fast separation process
- Minimal environmental impact

#### 4.5 Green Spectroscopic Methods

UV-Visible, FTIR, and NIR spectroscopy are all considered green since they do not require solvent use and provide fast analysis.

5. Phyto SAMPLE PREPARATION METHODS

Green sample preparation methods seek to minimize solvent use, waste, and reduce the time of analysis in pharmaceutical analysis. This is because green sample preparation methods are eco-friendly and improve lab safety.

Some Popular Green Sample Preparation Methods

Solid Phase Microextraction (SPME)

SPME is a solventless method that is widely used in pharmaceutical analysis.

Benefits:

- There is no need for an organic solvent
- Minimal amount of waste generated
- Sample preparation is quick

Microextration By Packed Sorbent (MEPS)

MEPS is a miniaturization of the extraction process using small volumes of solvent and sample.

Benefits:

- Low consumption of solvents
- Faster extraction process
- Eco-friendly method

<!-- page 9 -->

Microwave Assisted Extraction (MAE)

MAE employs microwaves to reduce extraction time.

Benefits:

- Reduced extraction time
- Reduced energy used
- Small amounts of solvents used

General Advantages

- Minimized hazardous waste
- Reduced costs
- Increased environmental safety
- Quicker pharmaceutical analysis
