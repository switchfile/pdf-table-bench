# **Building Bridges of Belonging: The Effect of Cultural Intelligence on the Employability of Tribal Youth in Madhya Pradesh** 

_Author 1: Shrishti Verma, Institute of Management Studies, DAVV, Indore, India Author 2: Dr. Nisha Bano Siddiqui, Institute of Management Studies, DAVV, Indore, India_ 

**Keywords:** Cultural Intelligence; Employability; Tribal Youth; Scheduled Tribes; Madhya Pradesh. 

## **Introduction** 

Cultural Intelligence (CQ) refers to the ability to function effectively across cultures and encompasses metacognitive, cognitive, motivational, and behavioral dimensions. CQ is a key predictor of employability and complements traditional skills and qualifications. Employability is a multidimensional concept that involves occupational expertise, adaptability, and social competence. 

Tribal youth in Madhya Pradesh, from Gond, Bhil, Baiga, Korku, and Sahariya communities, often face communication gaps and social distance in mainstream workplaces, which affect employability. 

This study examines how CQ affects tribal youth's employability and aims to inform interventions that support smoother workplace transitions. 

## **Literature Review** 

Cultural Intelligence Theory explains how metacognitive, cognitive, motivational, and behavioral capabilities support adaptation in new cultural settings like workplaces. 

Cultural Intelligence Theory suggests that an individual's capacity to function effectively in culturally diverse settings is shaped by metacognitive, cognitive, motivational, and behavioral capabilities that jointly determine adaptive behavior. Earley and Ang (2003) developed a framework widely used in cross-cultural management research to explain how individuals navigate unfamiliar cultural environments, including the workplace. 

Research shows CQ enhances adaptation and interpersonal skills in multicultural workplaces. CQ builds workplace confidence, but tribal youth still face training and workplace isolation barriers, highlighting the need for culturally responsive interventions. 

Higher CQ can improve communication, confidence, and job readiness for tribal youth. 

CQ development in education can increase employability and inclusion. 

## **Data and Methodology** 

A cross-sectional survey of 250 tribal youth will assess CQ and employability skills, supplemented by focus groups and interviews. Data analysis will include statistics and thematic analysis. 

The questionnaire will measure: 

- Metacognitive, cognitive, motivational, and behavioral Cultural Intelligence (CQS; Ang et al., 2007) 

- Occupational expertise, learning orientation, communication, and problem-solving skills. 

- Personal flexibility and career self-efficacy 

- Balance and overall employability (Van der Heijde & Van der Heijden, 2006) 

- Demographic and control variables: age, gender, tribe, education level, participation in skill development programs, and prior work exposure 

The study will use convenience sampling for focus group discussions and interviews to gain indepth insights. In contrast, random sampling for surveys will ensure statistical generalizability across the tribal youth population. Data analysis will utilize SPSS or Excel, applying descriptive statistics, correlation, and multiple regression to examine the predictive relationship between the four CQ dimensions and overall employability. 

## **Results and Discussion** 

Literature suggests motivational and behavioral CQ strongly predict employability. Metacognitive CQ helps individuals apply cultural knowledge, while cognitive CQ has less of an effect. CQ boosts confidence and adaptability, but cannot fully resolve structural barriers. 

Metacognitive CQ is expected to moderate the extent to which individuals apply cultural knowledge in workplace situations. In contrast, cognitive CQ, while foundational, may have a weaker independent effect because the population has limited prior exposure to formal crosscultural training. These anticipated patterns correspond with the structural barriers identified by Biswal et al. (2025) and extend the discussion of CQ as a psychological resource that can partially mitigate, but not fully resolve, the infrastructural and educational deficits experienced by tribal youth. 

## **Conclusion** 

CQ is a key factor in tribal youth employability. This study highlights CQ’s role in workplace readiness and adaptability. Integrating CQ into skill programs can improve job placement, retention, and inclusion. 

## **Managerial and Social Implications** 

Employers and policymakers should integrate CQ training into recruitment, onboarding, and skilling. This supports job placement, retention, and inclusion for tribal youth in Madhya Pradesh. 

At the societal level, enhancing CQ among tribal youth supports not only immediate job placement but also longer-term retention, career progression, and social inclusion, thereby linking individual readiness to broader societal outcomes.
