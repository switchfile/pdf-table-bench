Beyond One-Size-Fits-All Education: How Digital Technology and Teachers
Shape Personalized Learning
Jyoti Bhargad,
Dr Priyanka Vijay
Department of Commerce and Management Studies, Banasthali Vidhyapith, Tonk, India
Abstract
The rapid integration of digital technologies into education has transformed conventional
teaching and learning practices, creating new opportunities for flexible, learner-centred, and
personalized educational experiences. Digital platforms, online learning resources, adaptive
technologies, artificial intelligence-enabled tools, and virtual learning environments have
increasingly enabled students to access information, communicate, collaborate, receive
feedback, and engage with learning materials according to their individual needs and pace.
However, the availability and use of digital technologies alone may not necessarily result in
meaningful personalized learning. The effective integration of technology depends
considerably on how teachers guide students, interpret digital resources, provide feedback,
address individual learning needs, and facilitate the learning process. In this context, teacher
support can serve as an important link between technological resources and students’
personalized learning experiences. Therefore, this study examines the relationship between the
use of digital technology and personalized learning and specifically investigates the mediating
role of teacher support in this relationship.
The study is associated with constructivist learning, technology acceptance, and selfdetermination, the study proposes that digital technology can facilitate personalized learning by
providing students with greater access to learning resources, flexibility, interaction, feedback,
and opportunities to learn according to their individual requirements. At the same time, teachers
remain important facilitators who help students navigate digital resources, interpret feedback,
select appropriate learning materials, overcome learning difficulties, and develop meaningful
learning pathways. Accordingly, the study addresses three major relationships: the direct effect
of digital technology use on personalized learning, the effect of digital technology use on
teacher support, and the effect of teacher support on personalized learning. It further examines
whether teacher support mediates the relationship between digital technology use and
personalized learning.
A quantitative research design was adopted to empirically examine the proposed relationships.
The study was conducted among secondary school students from selected districts of Haryana,
namely Gurugram, Rewari, and Mahendergarh. The target population comprised students
studying in Classes 9 to 12. Data were collected using a structured questionnaire based on a
five-point Likert scale ranging from strongly disagree to strongly agree. A pilot study involving
50 respondents was conducted to assess the reliability and suitability of the questionnaire
before the final survey. Following the refinement of the instrument and removal of low-loading
indicators, 22 items were retained for the final analysis. The final sample consisted of 150

secondary school students, selected through stratified random sampling. The collected data
were analysed using Partial Least Squares Structural Equation Modelling (PLS-SEM) through
SmartPLS 4.1. The analysis included assessment of the measurement model and structural
model, followed by bootstrapping with 5,000 iterations to evaluate the significance of the
proposed relationships.
The findings provide significant empirical evidence for all the proposed relationships. First, the
use of digital technology has a positive and statistically significant effect on personalized
learning (β = 0.314, t = 3.614, p < 0.001). This finding indicates that the use of digital platforms
and technology-enabled learning resources can contribute to students’ opportunities to engage
with learning in ways that are more responsive to their individual requirements. Second, digital
technology use demonstrates a strong positive effect on teacher support (β = 0.655, t = 9.287, p
< 0.001). This suggests that technology can strengthen teachers’ capacity to provide guidance,
feedback, instructional resources, and individualized assistance to students. Third, teacher
support has a significant and positive effect on personalized learning (β = 0.609, t = 8.157, p <
0.001), highlighting the importance of teachers in transforming the technological potential of
digital learning into meaningful personalized educational experiences.
The mediation analysis confirms that teacher support significantly mediates the relationship
between digital technology use and personalized learning (β = 0.399, t = 7.541, p < 0.001). The
significant indirect effect demonstrates that digital technology contributes to personalized
learning not only through its direct influence but also by indirect teacher support provided.
Thus, teacher support functions as an important bridge between technological use and
personalized learning outcomes. The explanatory power of the structural model is also
substantial, with an R² value of 0.429 for teacher support and 0.720 for personalized learning,
indicating that the proposed model explains a meaningful proportion of variance in the
endogenous constructs. The measurement model further demonstrated satisfactory reliability
and validity, with composite reliability values above 0.85 and AVE values exceeding the
recommended threshold of 0.50. personalized.
The findings have important theoretical and practical implications. The study highlights the
importance of a human-centred approach to educational digital transformation in which
technology and teacher support work together to address diverse student needs and improve the
quality of personalized learning.
