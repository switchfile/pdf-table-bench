Introduction

Green finance has become an increasingly important tool for linking environmental objectives
with financial markets as governments and investors mobilise capital towards sustainable and
low-carbon   activities.   Green   bonds   are   becoming   a   crucial   financial   tool   for   funding
environmentally beneficial projects. The Greenium, a pricing or yield differential between
green bonds and similar traditional bonds, is a main research question in the field of green
bonds. The negative yield spread between green bonds and non-green bonds is often viewed as
investors'   willingness   to   sacrifice   the   financial   reward   of   investing   in   a   bond   for   the
environment.The rapid development of green bond markets has generated substantial academic
interest in Greenium and green bond pricing. Existing reviews have mainly used systematic
literature reviews, bibliometric analysis and conventional content analysis. These approaches
provide useful information on publications and results, but offer relatively limited insight into
the semantic and thematic structure of the Greenium literature.

Objective

This study aims to identify and map the major research themes in Greenium and green bond
pricing literature, examine their relative prominence and explore the relationships among the
identified topics.

Methodology

A thorough literature search was done by Scopus and Web of Science databases from 2016
through 2026, and it was updated till March 2026. A mixture of keywords relating to green
bonds and Greenium was used as a search strategy. Screening for subject-relevancy, document
type and English-language requirements and removing duplicate records leaving a total of 554
unique research articles. Bertopic was used to process titles, abstracts and keywords to detect
semantically coherent topics through document embeddings and clustering.

Results and Findings

The   BERTopic   analysis   shows   that   Greenium   and   green   bond   pricing   research   is
multidimensional   and   interconnected.   Nine   distinct   topics   were   identified,   with   Topic   0
representing the dominant research stream.

Topic 0, Greenium, premium, issuance and corporate financing, contains 203 publications,
making   it   the   largest   topic.   Representative   terms   include   greenium,   premium,   issuance,
corporate, issuer, yield, credit, liquidity, spread and financing.

Topic 1, oil shocks, oil prices and crude oil, comprises 60 publications and represents the link
between green financial assets and energy-market conditions.

Topic 2, renewable energy, emissions and resources, contains 57 publications. Emphasizing the
connection between green bond markets, financing for renewables and environmental goals.

Topic 3, hedging and hedging effectiveness, comprises 29 publications and includes hedge,
ESG, risk, diversification, correlations and optimal. This highlights that the focus is growing on
green bonds as portfolio assets.

Topic 4, clean-energy forecasting and machine learning, includes 23 publications and exhibits
methodological interest in forecasting and machine-learning methods.

Topic 5, climate policy and uncertainty, includes 20 publications and emphasises the relevance
of climate policy, climate risk and economic policy and transition risk.

Topic 6, Bitcoin, cryptocurrencies and gold, contains 20 publications and exhibits a broader
alternative-assets perspective.

Topic 7, spillovers, clean energy and environmental markets, contains 16 publications and
highlights relationships between clean energy, fossil fuels, carbon markets and ESG-related
financial assets.

Topic 8, spillover and contagion risk, comprises 10 publications and focuses on financial-
market transmission and interconnectedness.

The intertopic distance map and the topic similarity matrix suggest links between the themes of
pricing,   environmental,   energy   and   financial-market,   and   relative   separateness   between
alternative assets and contagion. Overall, the results show a core–periphery type structure. The
core is made up of greenium, yields, spreads, issuance, liquidity and credit characteristics,
whereas the peripheral themes are: renewable energy, oil markets, climate policy, portfolio
management, forecasting and financial spillovers.

Conclusion

This study examines 554 research articles and applies BERTopic to categorize the articles into
nine main topics, which shows the interdisciplinary growth of Greenium research. While bond
pricing is the focus, the literature also relates Greenium to renewable energy, emissions, oil-
price shocks, ESG, climate-policy uncertainty, portfolio strategies, forecasting, alternative
assets, and financial spillovers. The study complements the existing reviews by uncovering the
semantic structure and relationship between the research themes.

Limitations

The study is restricted to publications in Scopus and Web of Science up to March 2026. The size
of a topic does not always reflect the quality of research, its causal significance or its empirical
validity, and the interpretation of the topic depends in part on the judgement of the researcher.
BerTopic   might   be   integrated   with   systematic   review,   bibliometric   analysis   and   citation
analysis in future studies to get a complete picture of Greenium research.
